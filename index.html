About

A little background

Technology improves learning when it meets students' needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. The design is what makes the difference.

I am a Professor in Instructional Design and Technology at Bowling Green State University, where I also serve as Associate Dean for Faculty Affairs and Research. I earned my Ph.D. in Educational Psychology and Educational Technology from Michigan State University, and I have studied online learning environments since then.

My recent work looks at how students manage their own learning in online STEM courses. With funding from the National Science Foundation, I led CLAIM-STEM, a project that helped undergraduates build the math skills they need for STEM programs. I am now studying how leaderboards and other game elements change what students do and how they feel about it, and how a learning analytics dashboard can show students something useful about their own progress. I serve as Associate Editor for Educational Technology & Society and the Online Learning Journal.

Research

What I work on

I

Learning analytics

I look for patterns in what students do in online courses, and I design dashboards that show students their own progress in a form they can act on.

II

Gamification and simulation

I study how game elements such as leaderboards, and simulations such as virtual reality, change what students learn and how much they want to keep going.

III

Motivation and affect in online learning

I examine how online courses meet students' needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, and how the emotions students feel while working shape what they learn.

IV

AI in education

I investigate how students and instructors use AI tools, including the differences in how various groups take them up and what they think of them.
